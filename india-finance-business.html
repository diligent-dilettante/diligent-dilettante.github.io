a claude skill that reads indian company accounts

16 August 2026 · 5 min read · Sri Krishna Ravavarapu · code on GitHub

tl;dr: india-finance-business is a free, open-source Claude skill that reads an indian private company's accounts and tells you what they say. audited statements, Tally trial balances, Form 3CD, scanned PDFs. it tags what is audited and what is not, computes what growth costs in cash, and leaves out the ratios that need a share price. every figure comes from Python. it is the company-scope sibling of india-finance.

what india-finance-business is

it is a Claude skill for analysing an indian private limited company, LLP or proprietorship from the documents the business actually has, rather than from a clean dataset nobody ever has.

point it at a folder of statements and it builds a multi-year series, computes the ratios that apply to an unlisted company, works out the working capital cycle, and says which of its own numbers you should trust.

why generic financial tools break on indian company accounts

there are several competent financial-analyst skills already. they compute twenty ratios, run a DCF, do budget variance. they share four assumptions that do not hold here.

they assume a jurisdiction that does not exist. one states outright that it applies universal formulas without country-specific adjustment. an indian P&L runs on Schedule III of the Companies Act: revenue from operations, cost of materials consumed, employee benefits expense, finance costs. restructure that into revenue, COGS and opex and anyone official has to rebuild it before they can use it.

they assume clean input. real financials arrive as a scan of a signed statement, a Tally export somebody emailed, and a tax audit report nobody has opened. the ingestion is most of the work.

they treat every number as equally true. an audited figure and an unaudited trial balance are different kinds of fact.

and a quarter of their ratio set is dead on arrival. P/E, P/B, EV/EBITDA, PEG all need a market price. a private company has none. computing them anyway produces nulls, which look like missing data rather than an inapplicable question.

why provenance matters more than ratios

every year in the series carries a tag: audited, provisional, or trial balance. the tag follows the number into the output.

this sounds bureaucratic until it changes an answer. a Tally export prints its actual date range, and it is regularly something like 1-Apr-2021 to 11-Mar-2022. twenty days short. compare that to a full audited year and you will report a decline that never happened.

the same discipline catches the reverse. documents named bidder turnover or PQ pack are frequently not certificates at all, but several years of complete audited statements stapled together for a tender. an entire audited year can be sitting inside a file whose name suggests it holds one number.

two structural checks run before anything is computed. does the balance sheet balance. does the year-on-year movement in reserves equal profit after tax. the second is free and it is invaluable on a bad scan where the P&L is mangled but the balance sheet is legible.

why reported profit is not performance

a company that has just bought plant shows depreciation stepping up sharply. that depresses reported profit without touching cash. read profit alone and you conclude "flat" about a business whose cash generation is growing.

so the skill computes profit after tax plus depreciation alongside profit, across the whole series. revenue up 130%, profit up 50%, and profit plus depreciation up 140% is not a stagnant business. it is a business mid-capex, and the depreciation charge is doing the work.

the same reading rescues bad years. revenue can fall by a third while cash generation holds flat, which tells you the cost base is genuinely variable rather than that the company is in trouble.

what growth costs in cash

the working capital section is usually the most useful part of the output, and it is the part standard ratio sets under-serve.

receivable days, inventory days, payable days, and the cash conversion cycle. then net working capital as a percentage of revenue, which answers a question owners actually ask: what does another rupee of sales cost me before any profit arrives. a business sitting at 17% has to find seventeen paise of funding for every extra rupee it bills.

a negative cycle, where customer money lands before the supplier is paid, means growth funds itself. that is structurally safer than a margin advantage and it is worth saying out loud.

MSME status is not a footnote

for a company under the Udyam thresholds, classification is money. registered micro and small enterprises must be paid within 45 days under the MSMED Act, and past that, compound interest runs at three times the RBI bank rate and is not deductible for the buyer. in public procurement the status brings EMD exemption and purchase preference.

the skill classifies status, states what attaches, and flags the thing nobody models: these benefits are a function of being small and they erode as the business grows. a company whose tender access rests on MSE exemptions is standing on scaffolding that comes down at the threshold.

the document nobody opens

Form 3CD, the tax audit report, discloses things the financial statements do not. clause 23 lists payments to directors, relatives and associated enterprises. clause 31 lists every loan taken and repaid above the threshold, with the lender's name, the amount, and the maximum outstanding during the year.

track clause 31 across three years and inter-company funding becomes visible. a sister company appearing every year with rising amounts is a rolling credit line, not a loan. it is usually unsecured and uncommitted, and no ratio captures it.

Form 26AS does the other direction. it lists everyone who paid the company and deducted tax. divide the TDS by the rate and you recover what they billed. identify the section first: 2% under 194C and 0.1% under 194Q are a factor of twenty apart.

how to install india finance business

clone the repository and copy the skill folder into your Claude skills directory. 

git clone https://github.com/diligent-dilettante/india-finance.git
cp -r india-finance/india-finance-business ~/.claude/skills/

then ask it something. "read these statements and tell me what they say" is enough to start. it will ask which documents exist before it asks for anything else, because most people do not know what they have until they see the list.

questions

why not just use a generic financial analyst tool?

generic tools assume clean input and a listed company. indian SME financials arrive as scans, Tally exports and tax audit reports, and a quarter of a standard ratio set needs a share price that does not exist. the ingestion and the provenance are most of the work, and generic tools skip both.

what documents does it need?

audited statements for three to five years are the best start, and each carries a comparative column so one document gives two years. a recent trial balance adds currency. Form 3CD adds related-party detail. a Udyam certificate settles classification. it works with whatever subset exists.

does it work on scanned statements?

yes. scans are normal for anything before about 2018. it rasterises at 300 DPI before OCR, because lower resolution transposes digits silently, then validates every figure structurally rather than by eye.

why are P/E and EV/EBITDA missing?

they need a market price and an unlisted company has none. they are deliberately absent rather than reported as empty. if a valuation is genuinely needed it comes from a DCF or a transaction multiple, with the assumptions stated.

does it do personal finances too?

no. company scope only. india-finance covers one person's own money. separate skills, separate scopes, and neither reads the other's data.

is it free?

yes. MIT licensed, open source, nothing beyond existing Claude access. same repository as india-finance.
