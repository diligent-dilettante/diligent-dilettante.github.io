a claude skill that tells you where your money went

16 August 2026 · 4 min read · Sri Krishna Ravavarapu · code on GitHub

tl;dr: india-finance is a free, open-source Claude skill that reads your bank and card statements, tells you what changed, sizes what you own, and compares the two tax regimes on your real numbers. the arithmetic is Python, not a language model. nothing leaves your machine. MIT licensed. get it here.

every budgeting tool works until you feed it an Indian bank statement.

what india-finance is

india-finance is a Claude skill for one person's money in India. it parses bank and credit card statements in PDF, CSV or Excel, categorises the transactions, reports what moved, maps what you own, and compares the old and new tax regimes. it is free, MIT licensed, and runs on Claude web, Claude Desktop and Claude Code.

why the arithmetic is in Python

a language model doing mental maths on your savings rate will be wrong occasionally and confident always, and you will act on it.

so the split is deliberate. the model parses statements and interprets results. a deterministic script computes every number that reaches you. standard library only, nothing to install.

why generic tools break on indian statements

the parsing is where most of them quietly fail, and the failures are silent rather than loud.

Indian statements carry separate debit and credit columns instead of one signed amount, so a naive import flips the sign on half your ledger and the totals still look plausible. dates are DD/MM/YYYY, which a US-defaulting parser reads month-first and mis-buckets a quarter of the year. digits group in lakhs. UPI narrations bury the counterparty behind an aggregator handle, so half your spending resolves to "Paytm". and credit card statements invert the sign convention, which books every card purchase as income.

it also refuses to analyse a ledger that doesn't reconcile against the statement's own running balance. an analysis missing four percent of your transactions is worse than useless, because it looks complete.

what it reports

the delta, not the level. you already know roughly what you earn and spend. you don't know what moved.

category movement against a trailing average, the largest movers in rupees rather than percent, recurring charges annualised, concentration in your top five merchants, and how much of your outflow is cash and therefore invisible.

recommendations carry a number. "reduce dining out" is not advice. "delivery averaged ₹18,400 a month over the last six against ₹11,200 in the six before, and the whole increase sits in three apps" is advice, because it names the thing and the number. three at most, ranked by rupees.

what you own, not just what you spend

flow is the easy half. most Indian household wealth sits in property, gold and a family business, and almost nobody has the allocation written down anywhere.

property is the awkward one, because it carries three different values. the circle rate, the documented value on the deed, and what actually changed hands. net worth has to use market value or every percentage is wrong. capital gains at sale are computed against the documented value, so where those two diverge the taxable gain is larger than the real one. people find this out at the point of sale, which is too late to plan around.

the skill records all three, labelled, and flags the gap. it also computes liquidity, because someone with a large net worth and three weeks of accessible cash has a problem the net worth figure hides entirely.

it will not pick funds or call the market. what it does is arithmetic and structure: where you're concentrated, what a goal actually requires, and what borrowing against illiquid assets costs. for security selection, a SEBI-registered adviser.

tax

old regime against new, computed on your numbers rather than reasoned about, both totals side by side. the slabs are dated in the repo and it verifies them with you before computing, because tax law changes at every Budget and a stale slab produces a confidently wrong answer.

that's for planning. for actually filing, it hands off to file-itr, which is purpose-built and parses Form 16, AIS and 26AS. capital gains, foreign income or business income want a CA.

how to install india finance

copy the skill folder in:

cp -r india-finance ~/.claude/skills/

on Claude web, upload it as a skill or drop it into a project. then say what you want: "here are my statements, where did my money go last quarter", "am I saving enough", or "which tax regime should I be on". first run is about 45 minutes, mostly categorising. it saves what it learns, so later runs take ten.

questions

why compute in Python instead of using the model?

a language model doing arithmetic on your savings rate will be wrong occasionally and confident always. the model parses and interprets; a script does the maths.

why do generic budgeting tools fail on indian statements?

separate debit and credit columns, DD/MM/YYYY dates read month-first by US-defaulting parsers, lakh digit grouping, UPI narrations that hide the merchant behind an aggregator, and card statements with an inverted sign convention.

does it handle indian income tax?

it compares old and new regimes on your actual numbers, for planning. slabs are dated and verified with you first. filing hands off to a purpose-built skill, and anything beyond salary wants a CA.

is my financial data uploaded anywhere?

no. local only, no uploads, no web calls carrying transaction data, and it declines to write balances into any shared store. full account numbers are never recorded.

does it work if you're not salaried?

yes. it asks four questions first and adapts. freelancers get 44ADA presumptive taxation and advance tax raised, since no employer is deducting. retired users get the age-based exemption limits and 80TTB. anyone paying their parents' health premiums gets the separate 80D limit for that, which is routinely unclaimed. a shop owner whose personal and business money share one account is told the analysis is unreliable rather than handed a confident wrong number.

does it work for a business?

no. individual scope. a family business appears only as your exposure to it: a stake, an income stream, and any personal guarantee sitting behind its debt. that last one is the item people forget and the one that matters most. for the company's own accounts there is a sibling skill, a skill for the company's own finances.

is it free?

yes. MIT licensed, open source, nothing needed beyond existing Claude access.
