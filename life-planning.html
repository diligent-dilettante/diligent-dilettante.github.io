a life planning skill built for week three

August 2026 · Sri Krishna Ravavarapu · code on GitHub

tl;dr: life-planning is a free, open-source Claude skill that runs values, direction, goals and a ten-minute weekly check-in as a conversation. it keeps one markdown file and does the tracking, so nothing has to be maintained. MIT licensed. get it here.

almost nobody fails at the planning session. that part is enjoyable.

plans die in week three. nothing gets looked at, something slips, and the slip reads as evidence the whole idea was naive. a year later the same person tries again with a better system, which fails the same way.

what life planning is

life-planning is a Claude skill for one person working out what they want and what they are going to do about it. it asks questions rather than handing over templates. everything it learns goes into a single markdown file called life-plan.md, so sessions compound instead of restarting. it is free, MIT licensed, and runs on Claude web, Claude Desktop and Claude Code.

it covers values, direction, twelve-week goals, the commitments a goal has to sit on, money, who carries what at home, and getting unstuck. one person is the design centre. a partner is an optional layer that adds a question worth taking home, and the skill assumes that question never comes back answered.

valuespeak moments, disproportionate irritation, and what each value costs. 20 minutes, or 90.
visionworkview and lifeview, odyssey plans, an ordinary Tuesday three years out. 30 minutes, or 90.
goalstwelve-week goals, WOOP, if-then plans, lead measures. 25 minutes, or 60.
foundationswhat a goal is standing on. runway, commitments, the rung that is missing. 20 minutes.
check-inthe weekly one. your numbers read back, one number in, anything in the way. 10 minutes.
reviewquarterly scoring and the annual past-year review. 30 minutes.
stuckimmunity to change, a jammed decision, the argument that keeps recurring. 45 minutes.
money and loadmoney scripts and household load. 45 minutes.

how it works

every session after the first opens with Claude reading the file and saying where things stand before being asked. what was set, what got logged, what has not been touched in a month. flatly. no praise for a good number and no concern about a bad one, because the moment tracking sounds like judgement the numbers being reported start being wrong.

the weekly measure is whatever the person said in answer to one question: what is the thing you fully control that you could do this week. a number invented for someone is one they fail against without having agreed to it.

three quarters in, the file knows things its owner does not. which goals get finished and which get carried. that both stalls landed in the fortnight after a work trip. that a goal with a weekly number attached gets done and one without does not.

you never open a spreadsheet. a system that needs you to be organised has already failed the person who most needs it.

how is it different from other planning skills

the published alternatives are template systems. they hand over the frameworks and the documents, and assume the hard part is knowing what to write down.

the hard part is week three. most of this skill's length sits there and on how the conversation is run. the file on conversational stance is the largest in the repo and contains no frameworks at all. it is mostly about staying quiet, following the flinch rather than the content, and resisting the urge to fix.

one consequence: when the log shows a goal stalled right after a work trip, the skill does not say so before asking why. offer someone a ready-made external cause and they will take it, sincerely, and the conversation ends there. the open question comes first and the pattern comes later, if it still matters.

the same instinct governs dropping. most people cannot drop a goal, because dropping feels like failing, so they carry it dead for another quarter and it discredits everything next to it. the skill asks directly whether the goal is still wanted, or whether what is wanted is not to be the person who dropped it.

what happens after a lapse

a month of silence is the normal case. going back does not require tidying up first. the skill reads back where things were, asks whether it was a bad month or whether the goal was never the one, halves whatever was being attempted, and starts again in about twenty minutes. there is no autopsy. a miss is data, and the skill says so out loud before the first one happens.

how it was tested

six single-turn evaluations against no-skill baselines, then seven multi-turn conversations run between isolated agents. one agent played a person who did not know a skill existed. another ran the skill without knowing the user was simulated. a third relayed between them and wrote the critique.

each persona was built to attack a specific failure mode: someone whose partner is not interested, someone demanding a full system on day one, someone deflecting a lapse with a plausible excuse, someone for whom a values session turns into a marriage in trouble, someone about to leave a salary for a business.

thirty-two defects came out of it. the useful ones were not bugs in the exercises. they were rules that contradicted each other, and habits that felt warm and were not: grading a disclosure, announcing the mechanic out loud, reassurance dressed up as a statistic, handing someone their own words back sharpened.

how to install life planning

on Claude web, turn on code execution first, then upload the skill:

Settings → Capabilities → Code execution and file creation
Customize → Skills → + → Create skill → upload life-planning.zip

on Claude Code, copy the folder in:

cp -r life-planning ~/.claude/skills/

then describe what is going on, in ordinary words: "help me work out what actually matters to me", "i made a plan in january and nothing happened", or "let's do my check-in". the skill picks the smallest session that fits and starts there. it does not open with a menu.

questions

who is it for?

one person, in their own conversation, over months. that is the design centre rather than a fallback, and everything works if they are the only one who ever uses it. someone with a partner gets an optional layer: one question per session worth asking them, and a note of what the partner has and has not seen.

does it work without a partner?

yes, completely. the skill was rewritten around the single-user case after testing showed partner-shaped scaffolding leaking through. the belonging rung, friendships and community, gets watched precisely because there is no partner in the room pointing at it.

how long is a session?

twenty minutes for the first. ten minutes a week after that. thirty minutes once a quarter. the long versions exist and are usually the wrong choice.

where does the data live?

one markdown file, wherever you keep it. nothing gets uploaded anywhere, nothing is locked in, and the whole thing reads in five minutes.

do i need Claude Code?

no. it runs on Claude web with code execution enabled, which is where most people will use it.

is it therapy?

no, and it says so itself. if what surfaces is bigger than a planning conversation, it names that once, plainly, and then goes back to the planning work rather than continuing to excavate. it is a coaching tool for goals and direction, not a substitute for professional care.

is it free?

yes. MIT licensed, open source, nothing needed beyond existing Claude access.

will it write the plan for you?

no. it asks and writes down the answers. a section that was not earned in the conversation is left blank rather than filled in to look finished, because a plan someone was handed is one they will not recognise in November.

credits

the exercises are established work, assembled and adapted. GROW, the wheel of life, the ACT bull's eye, workview and lifeview and odyssey plans from designing your life, WOOP and implementation intentions, the twelve week year, lead and lag measures, immunity to change, solution-focused questions, the gottman state of the union, berger's running order for a marriage meeting, eve rodsky's fair play, money scripts, tim ferriss's past year review. full attributions are in the repo.

the stance file, which is the one that matters, comes from how good coaches and therapists behave rather than from any framework.
